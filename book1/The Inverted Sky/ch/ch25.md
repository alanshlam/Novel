# Chapter 25: The Price of Greed  

Eighteen months of stalling the war machine had bought Hopewell a fragile peace, but now the order had come: reassignment. As he stood at the edge of the thriving county he had helped build, watching Eva Rostova take command and Sofia Frost’s determined gaze linger on him one last time, Sean felt the weight of fragile hope slipping through his fingers. Thousands of broken refugees remained trapped in camps outside Haven-4 colony, held hostage by one man’s greed. But in the encrypted shadows of the Weather Forum, Ruby’s voice cut through like a blade: “We end this today.” The machine had finally pushed too far — and Sean was ready to push back.

Greedy, arrogant, and quick to bluster when threatened, Grim embodies the worst of the machine: a man who profits from suffering and crumbles when his hidden wealth and connections are exposed.

<summary> Historical Note: Governments Refusing Their Own Citizens</summary>           

Throughout history, governments have occasionally refused, delayed, or actively blocked the return of their own citizens, often through denaturalization, statelessness policies, or political expediency. In the 20th century, Nazi Germany stripped Jews and political opponents of citizenship, rendering many stateless and unable to return. The Soviet Union and other authoritarian regimes similarly revoked citizenship from dissidents and exiles. In modern times, the United States and other nations have denaturalized individuals for fraud or crimes, sometimes leaving them stateless if their country of origin refuses repatriation. Deportation cases frequently stall when origin countries decline to issue documents, leaving people in legal limbo. While rare in stable democracies, such actions often serve political punishment, bureaucratic avoidance of responsibility, or strategic denial during conflicts — underscoring how citizenship can become a tool of power rather than a fundamental right. In times of war or crisis, the human cost of these decisions is profound, turning citizens into unwanted ghosts between borders.

## Farewell to Fragile Hope 
    

Eighteen months had passed since his promotion, and for eighteen months, Sean had played a dangerous game of stalling. The Combine High Command had been relentless in their demands that he push his troops toward the Fredericton frontline, but Sean always had the same answer: the base was compromised. He insisted on rooting out "moles" before moving a single soldier, warning that any premature advance would be a gift to the Federation.   

The moles, of course, didn't exist. They were phantoms—calculated fabrications created by Ruby to provide a convincing shield. Eventually, however, the High Command’s patience evaporated. The order arrived not as a request, but as a mandate: *Major Sean Walker is hereby reassigned to Colony Haven-4 under Colonel Ivan Rostova. Major Eva Rostova will assume command of the Hopewell base effective immediately.*   

Sean stood at the shuttle dock as Eva’s transport settled onto the pad, the whine of its engines cutting through the morning mist. She stepped down in full combat fatigues, older now, the lines of command etched deeper into her face. They met in a brief, soldier’s embrace — firm, wordless, the kind shared by those who had dragged each other from hell more than once.   

"After Dunfeld, I thought you'd finally run out of miracles," Eva said, a small smile playing on her lips. "But it seems you've worked another miracle here in Hopewell. A county. You built something real."  

"I didn't have much choice," Sean replied dryly. "The alternative was not being here to greet you."   

Eva’s expression turned serious. "As your successor, I'll follow your lead. I'll keep chasing these 'moles,' real or imagined, and I will protect everything you've built here. You saved my brother twice, Sean. I owe you this. I won't let you down."  

"I know you won't," Sean said. "That's why I'm glad it was you they sent. Your reputation precedes you—specifically the way you handled Corvus’s column back in Erden for saving a village. Come, let me introduce you to Sofia Frost. She’s the liaison for the local government; you two will be a formidable team."  

After a brief meeting in Sean's office with Sofia, Eva surprisingly agreed to move the base fifty kilometers east to allow Hopewell more room to grow, it was time for Sean to say his goodbyes.   

The hardest goodbye waited at the main gate. Sofia Frost stood waiting, arms crossed against the chill, her dark hair catching the light like it had the first day they met. The town behind her — rebuilt streets, bustling markets, children playing where rubble once lay — was a living testament to what they had fought for. But her eyes held the knowledge that the fight was never truly over.
Sean walked Sofia toward the gates of the base. The air between them felt heavy; Sofia had been quiet since learning of his departure.   

"Don't look so grim," Sean told her gently. "Major Rostova is an exceptional officer. She's professional, capable, and fair. You're in good hands."   

Sofia stopped and looked up at him. "I believe that—I can tell after meeting her. But..." she hesitated, "if something happens... something she can't handle... would you come back for us?"   

"Trust Eva first," Sean advised. "She is more than enough to protect this city. But if the need ever becomes absolute, I will help her."   

"Can I see you again?" Sofia asked softly. "Maybe in Erden?"   

Sean looked toward the horizon, his mind already drifting toward the uncertainty of Colony Haven-4. "I don't know where I'm headed yet. But if there's a chance, I'll send word."  

Suddenly, Sofia gripped his hand, her fingers tightening as she locked her eyes onto his. "Promise me, Sean. Promise that you will help Hopewell again if we need it."  

Sean nodded slowly. He gave her the promise because he wanted to, but as he looked at her, a cold sense of doubt lingered in the back of his mind. He would try his best, but he couldn't guarantee anything. In a world like this, he wasn't even sure if Hopewell had a future to be saved.  

He watched her walk back toward the town gates, the morning light catching the new buildings, the bustling life. For a heartbeat, it felt like victory. Then the shuttle’s engines began their low growl behind him, and the weight returned.  


As the craft lifted off, Sean stared down at the shrinking valley. Hopewell looked small from the sky — vulnerable, beautiful, and impossibly precious. He had given his word. But in eighteen months of stalling, he had learned a bitter truth: some promises were heavier than any rank or medal.  

And in the cold calculus of war, even the strongest hopes could be broken.

## The Rot Beneath the Surface  

The Haven-4 military base smelled of recycled air, gun oil, and the faint, metallic tang of too many desperate bodies. Eighteen months after leaving Hopewell, Sean Walker stepped into Colonel Ivan "Ox" Rostova’s office and felt something loosen in his chest for the first time in weeks.  

Sean found Ox in his office at the Haven-4 military base. It was a relief to see him fully recovered; the dullness was gone from his eyes, and he carried himself with the familiar, imposing strength Sean remembered.  

"Walker," Ox rumbled, clasping Sean’s forearm in a grip that still felt like iron. "You look like hell. But you brought me back from it once. Figured I owed you a brigade."  

"Thanks for bringing me into the brigade," Sean said, glancing around the facility. " This place is a damn sight more organized than Hopewell was when we first met. Cleaner, at least."  

Ox’s smile was grim, the kind that never reached the eyes. "Cleaner on the surface." He gestured to a holo-display flickering above his desk — thousands of red markers clustered in makeshift camps. "Different kind of rot here."   

The display zoomed. Rows of tents stretched across a windswept plain. Malnourished figures shuffled between them. Medics moved like ghosts among the worst cases. Sean’s jaw tightened.   

Ox leaned over his desk, his expression darkening. "Thousands of colony citizens are sitting in our camps. When the Scourge of Kassian seized Haven-4 two years ago, he took thousands of hostages back to his stronghold. We liberated them last month, but they're shells of people—malnourished, tortured, and sick. We don’t have the facilities here to keep them alive much longer. They need to go home."   

Sean studied the images — a young mother clutching a skeletal child, her belly swollen. Another woman, barely more than a girl, rocking back and forth with vacant eyes. The weight settled on him like the gravity of a dying world.   

"I can handle the transport," he said, voice steady. "Give me the manifest. I’ll prioritize the critical cases."  

Ox shook his head. "If it were just logistics, I wouldn’t need *you*. The problem is Governor General Thaddeus Grim."  

The name landed like a slap. Sean’s eyes narrowed.  

"He’s blocked every transport," Ox continued. "Claims the refugees are ‘compromised’ — sleeper agents brainwashed by the Scourge. Says bringing them back is inviting the enemy through the front door. And of course, the colony is suddenly ‘too broke’ to feed its own people."   

Sean stiffened. "They're his own citizens. Where was Grim when the Scourge actually attacked?"  

"Sipping wine in the capital, Vanguard " Ox replied coldly. "Fled at the first sign of trouble. Left his people to be slaughtered or taken. Now that we’ve saved them, he wants them to disappear again."   

The holo flickered with another image — Grim at some lavish reception, smiling for the cameras while the camps rotted. Sean felt the old familiar rage stir, the same fire that had driven him in Erden.  

"Consistent bastard," he muttered. "Abandons them to die, then refuses to take them back”.  
 
Sean stared at the holo — at the pregnant women, the hollow-eyed children. For a moment, the polished walls of the office faded. He saw Hopewell’s early days again: the starving villagers, Sofia’s fierce defiance, the slow, painful birth of something worth protecting.  

"I’ll get them home," he said, the words a quiet vow. "One way or another."  

Ox studied him for a long beat, then nodded once. "That’s why you’re here, Major. Be careful with Grim. He has friends in high places.  I'm working with a few senators who still have a conscience to push the paperwork through, but for now, we're setting up a vetting center. We'll screen them one by one and hope the capital Vanguard caves."

**One Week Later**   

The urgency in Ox’s office had thickened like smoke.   

"I’ve prepped the first wave," Sean reported, pacing. "Seven hundred and fifty women and children. One hundred and fifty of them pregnant. They can’t stay in those camps any longer. The medics are already losing people. Has the capital blinked?"  

Ox shook his head, rubbing his temples. "Grim’s dug in like a tick. Even with Federation media broadcasting the camps across the sector, High Command is dismissing it as enemy propaganda."  

Sean had already looped Ruby into the situation, asking her to amplify the scandal and squeeze the Combine from the outside, but the bureaucracy was proving unexpectedly resilient.  

"My sources say Grim is playing a game," Ox continued. "He’s using those refugees as bargaining chips. He's demanding more reconstruction funds from Vanguard, claiming he can't take the citizens back until his budget is increased."  

Sean stopped pacing. The numbers didn’t add up. "The reconstruction budget for Haven-4 was *billions*. Most of the infrastructure survived because the Scourge thought they’d be coming back for it. Where the hell did the money go?"  

Ox met his gaze, the silence heavy with implication. "That’s the question of the hour. A few senators with consciences are asking the same thing. But inconvenient questions have a way of disappearing around Grim."  

Sean looked out the window toward the distant camps. The wind whipped across the plains, carrying the faint, haunting sound of a child crying. His fists clenched at his sides.  

The machine was the same everywhere.  

But so was the fight.

## Ghosts in the Static

The encrypted channel they called the “Weather Forum” flickered with static, a digital storm mirroring the one brewing on the ground.  

"I can't stand it," Sean’s voice crackled through, raw with frustration. "People are dying in those camps because one man is too greedy to let them go home."   


"Believe me," Ruby replied, her voice a calm counterpoint across the void, "I feel the same. But I’ve scanned every financial trail in Cygnus for the last six months. Nothing. No spikes. No anomalies."  

Sean leaned back in the dim light of his quarters, the glow of the holo-display casting hard shadows across his face. "He’s not sloppy. He’s using thousands of proxies to scrub the money. If it’s not in Cygnus, he’s playing a deeper game." He paused, eyes narrowing. "Ruby... go into the Stark network. The backdoor we left open. Dig through the deep state archives. Find his vault."    

A beat of silence. Then Ruby’s voice returned, edged with steel. "I’m on it. But be careful, Sean. Grim isn’t just a governor. He’s a political animal with claws in the capital. Watch your back."

## Line in the Dust

**One Week Later**

The dust of the borderlands rose in choking clouds as Sean led the first convoy of refugees toward Haven-4. Hundreds of hollow-eyed survivors — women clutching children, pregnant mothers swaying with exhaustion — shuffled behind the armoured vehicles. The air tasted of despair.  

A line of armoured vehicles blocked the road. Lieutenant Colonel Sharon Sterling stepped forward, her uniform crisp, her expression carved from ice.  

"Major Walker," she called, voice carrying over the wind. "Turn your convoy around. No one enters without Governor General Grim’s explicit authorization."  

Sean stepped out, offering a crisp salute. "Then let me speak with the General."   

In a blur of motion, Sterling’s sidearm was drawn, levelled at his chest. "The General is unavailable. Turn back now... or we will use force."  

Behind Sean, his escort bristled, hands drifting toward holsters. The tension crackled like lightning. One wrong word, and the road would run red.   

Sean raised a hand, signalling his men to stand down. He didn't want a bloodbath; he wanted a victory. He reached slowly into his jacket and pulled out a single sheet of paper, holding it out to Sterling.    

"Pass this manifest to General Grim," he said, voice low and steady. "Tell him it’s critical military intelligence. Tell him... he cannot afford to ignore it."   

Sterling snatched the paper, glancing at it. Her eyes widened. She expected troop movements or coordinates. Instead, she saw a meticulously detailed list: luxury villas in the coastlands, super-yachts, offshore casino accounts, rare antiquities, and a web of shell companies.  

"Is this some kind of joke?" she snapped.  

"Just show it to him," Sean replied, his gaze unblinking. "Then let him decide if he wants to see me."

## The Vault Opens

The atmosphere in General Grim’s office was suffocating. Sean stood at attention, having delivered a formal salute that Grim ignored. The General didn't even offer him a seat; he simply stared at the paper on his desk as if it were a venomous snake.
   
"Explain this," Grim demanded, voice tight.  

"One third of those assets are already frozen," Sean said casually. He checked his watch, the ticking sound filling the silence like a countdown. "As we speak, an investigation into money laundering is underway. If my convoy isn't through the gates in the next five minutes, the rest of these accounts will be frozen and seized by the Federation government."   

Grim’s face went pale. He bit his lip, his eyes darting. "I have no idea what you're talking about."   

"At the bottom of that list," Sean continued, stepping closer, "are your shadow proxies. The people who actually move the money for you. They’ve already been contacted. They are more than happy to turn state's evidence and bury you in exchange for fifty percent of your stolen Haven-4 funds."   

Grim’s hands trembled. For the first time, he looked at Sean not as a mere Major, but as something far more dangerous.
"Who *are* you?" he whispered.    

Just a friend of Victor Stark," Sean lied smoothly, the name landing like a hammer. "You were either very brave or incredibly stupid to hide your loot in Federation territory. I assume you have ‘connections’ there? Or maybe... you’re looking for a way to defect?"  

Grim was speechless. The color had fled his face entirely. He looked at the manifest, then at the clock, then back at Sean — a man cornered by his own greed.  

"If I were you," Sean whispered, leaning in, "I would act *very* wisely. Let me pass."

## Justice in the Quiet

Two weeks later, the morning sun streamed through Sean's window as he sat with a cup of coffee and a news podcast.  

*"...in a triumphant return, all hostages captured by the Scourge have been safely repatriated to Haven-4," the announcer beamed. "Furthermore, several local NGOs have reported receiving massive anonymous donations to provide medical care for the returning citizens..."*  

Sean smiled. "Good work, Ruby."  

*"In other news, General Thaddeus Grim has announced his early retirement. He plans to relocate to Cygnus for a quiet life of leisure. Colonel Ivan Rostova will assume the role of Acting Governor of Haven-4."*  

Later that day, Sean logged back into the Weather Forum.  

"How much did you leave him?" Sean asked.  

"Just enough to make the trip," Ruby replied, a note of dark satisfaction in her voice. "The moment he settles into his new villa in Cygnus, I’m draining every last credit from those accounts and sending it straight to the NGOs. He’ll be living in a shelter by winter."  

Sean laughed softly. "He deserves every bit of it."  

"Now," Ruby said, tone shifting to something quieter, more hopeful, "work with Ivan. Make Haven-4 a place where people can actually live."  

"I will," Sean replied, gazing out at the horizon where the camps were slowly emptying. "The world would be a much more beautiful place if there were more Ivans... and fewer Grims."   

He closed the channel, but the words lingered. For now, the refugees were home. For now, Hopewell and Haven-4 had breathing room.  

But Sean knew the machine never truly stopped turning. Somewhere out there, new shadows were already gathering.  

And when they came, he would be ready.
